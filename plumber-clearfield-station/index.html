What Clearfield Station is

Clearfield City describes Clearfield Station as a 56-acre transit-oriented development around the FrontRunner stop at about 1200 S State Street. The plan calls for up to 1,000 homes, mostly apartments and townhomes, plus about 550,000 square feet of office space, about 67,500 square feet of commercial space and a 2.25-acre park.

News coverage reported that roads, utilities and the park were finished by October 2024, and that a first phase of 213 apartments was expected to start around spring 2025. Property managers can say which buildings are open today.

What is verified here, and what is not

The size, location and planned mix above come from the city's development project page and from KSL's report on the project. Both describe plans, and plans change.

Clearfield Station is new, so this page makes no claim about old pipes or worn plumbing, and no source describes the plumbing inside any specific building. The plumber looks at the actual system at your address.

Source: clearfield.city current development projects and KSL, article 51155621.

Who to call first: tenant, owner or manager

Most homes here are apartments and townhomes, so the first question is who controls the pipe. A leak under your own sink is usually yours to report. A stain on a ceiling can come from a shared line or from the unit above, and that is the building owner's to chase.

Report a leak to your property manager or association before you book a plumber, because many leases and association rules decide who may hire one. If the manager says to go ahead, or you own the unit, tap to call.

Services residents ask about

A new fixture, a dishwasher hookup or an added line falls under plumbing installation. A sink that will not hold hot water is a water heater repair question.

A damp wall or an unexpected bill points toward leak detection. Offices in the district can read the commercial plumbing page.

Permits and who to ask

Clearfield's Business and Development page says most repairs or alterations require a permit. It does not single out any particular job, so we do not state a rule. Ask Clearfield City Community Development at City Hall, 55 S State Street, and ask the plumber how the permit will be handled.

Next to Downtown Clearfield

Clearfield Station is one of three revitalization sites the city names for its downtown area, with Lakeside Square and Lotus Anthem. The Downtown Clearfield page covers the core around City Hall. For an address across the city line, the Layton page covers that neighboring city.

Questions about plumbing at Clearfield Station

Where is Clearfield Station?

The city places it around the FrontRunner stop at about 1200 S State Street in Clearfield. It is a 56-acre district. Give the exact building address when you call.

Is Clearfield Station finished?

Not as a whole. The plan covers up to 1,000 homes and office and commercial space built in phases. Ask the developer or your property manager which buildings are open.

I rent here. Should I call a plumber myself?

Tell your property manager first. Many leases decide who may hire a plumber, and a leak can involve lines you do not control. If the manager approves, call us.

Do you work on new buildings?

Yes. Repairs, fixtures and water heaters are handled the same in new buildings as old ones, and the plumber inspects what is actually installed at your address.

Can you take a commercial call from the office space?

Yes. Office and shop plumbing is covered on the commercial plumbing page. Check your lease to see whether the landlord or the tenant handles repairs.

Do I need a permit for plumbing work in Clearfield?

Clearfield says most repairs or alterations require a permit. For a specific job, ask Clearfield City Community Development at City Hall.
