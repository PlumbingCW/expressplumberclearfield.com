Emergency Faucet Repair in Clearfield, UT

Faucet repair in Clearfield, UT means fixing the faucet, the valve behind it, and the supply line feeding it, so water stops dripping, spraying, or running where it should not. Burst supply valve: stop the water first

The small chrome stop valve and braided line under a sink are the parts that burst most often. They sit in a cabinet, out of sight, so a split can run for hours before you see a swollen cabinet floor. Water at that point is under full house pressure and does not stop on its own.

Close the main shutoff, or the stop valve if it still turns, then open the faucet to drain the line. Pull everything out of the cabinet, keep power strips and outlets out of any wet area, and take photographs of the damage for your insurer.

Night or weekend, the same applies. The dispatch line stays open, and a same-day appointment is offered when one is available, so you are not waiting for a Monday slot while the cabinet soaks.

Which kind of faucet are you dealing with

The repair depends on the mechanism inside the handle. Telling the plumber the type, even roughly, speeds up the first visit.

Compression faucets with separate hot and cold handles

These use a rubber washer pressed against a seat. When the washer hardens, the faucet drips from the spout, and a worn seat can score the new washer. Replacing both usually ends it.

Single-handle cartridge and ball faucets

A cartridge or an O-ring set wears out, and then the spout drips or water seeps from the base of the handle. These are often repaired with a new cartridge rather than a new faucet.

Ceramic disc faucets

The discs last a long time, but sediment can scratch them. If a disc faucet leaks, the plumber checks the seals and the inlet screens before suggesting replacement.

Freeze nights and the outdoor spigot

NOAA's 1991-2020 normals for the Ogden-area station show about 121 nights a year with lows at or below 32 degrees, and January alone has 28.7 of them. A hose bibb left connected to a hose can freeze and split behind the wall, and you may not see it until the first warm weekend when you turn it on.

If a spigot sprays water inside the wall or the basement ceiling, close the interior shutoff for that line first. A frost-proof bibb has a long stem so the shutoff seat sits inside the heated space, and replacing a split one is normally a fixture-level repair.

Source: NOAA NCEI 1991-2020 Climate Normals, Ogden Hinckley Airport station (nearest normals station, not Clearfield itself).

Signs a faucet needs more than a washer

A drip at the spout is the obvious one, but a faucet often warns in other ways. Look for water on the cabinet floor, a handle that is stiff or loose, low flow from one side only, or a green crust at a connection.

A hissing sound after you shut the faucet usually points at a worn seat or a failing stop valve behind it.

Weber Basin Water Conservancy District, which supplies most of Clearfield's water, reports district-wide hardness of 10 to 18 grains per gallon. Clearfield City's own report lists no hardness figure, so test your tap. If mineral crust builds on aerators and cartridges, expect shorter part life and ask about a water softener.

Why the adopted code still matters for a faucet

Replacing a faucet is fixture work that needs no city permit, according to Clearfield's permit guidance. Clearfield City's 2026 design criteria list the 2024 International Plumbing Code as the adopted plumbing code, effective July 1, 2026.

The code matters at the supply stops and the drain connection. If a stop valve will not shut fully, replace it during the visit, because a faucet job that leaves a failing stop behind has not fixed the real weak point.

How a plumber diagnoses a leak at a faucet

The plumber first closes the stops and confirms they hold, because a valve that does not shut makes every other repair messy. Then the handle comes off, the cartridge or washer comes out, and each wear surface is checked.

Pressure matters too. A small gauge on an outdoor spigot shows if the house runs high, which wears seals faster. If the faucet is fine and the leak is in the wall, the plumber stops and tells you, since a faucet visit cannot pinpoint a hidden pipe leak.

The EPA says a faucet dripping once a second can waste more than 3,000 gallons a year. It also suggests checking your meter before and after two hours of no water use to catch hidden leaks. That test costs nothing and narrows the problem fast.

Repair, replace, or leave alone

Many faucets can be repaired for the price of one part. Others are better replaced, particularly when finishes are failing or parts are no longer made.

Shower valves and tub drains

A shower valve that drips from the showerhead after you turn it off has a worn cartridge or seats, and the plumber can usually replace the cartridge from the front without opening the wall. A drain that slowly empties is a different job, often a hair clog in the tub strainer or trip lever, and drain cleaning covers stubborn ones.

A bathtub faucet that will not shut off

A tub spout that keeps running after the handle is closed wastes water fast, and you hear it in the next room. The diverter, the cartridge, and the valve body all take turns being the cause.

The plumber confirms the shutoff first, then replaces the worn part. If the valve body itself is corroded inside the wall, access is needed, and that is quoted before work starts.

Faucet repair or new fixture installation

Clearfield City says replacing plumbing fixtures does not need a permit, while new or replacement plumbing lines do. Swapping a faucet, then, needs no permit. Moving a sink or adding a line is a different job, so ask the Clearfield City building department.

Utah licensing law exempts minor fixture repair in a home, but not work that changes culinary water, drain, or vent piping. Any kitchen with a failing disposal should also read about garbage disposal repair before a new sink goes in.

When the faucet is not the real problem

If every faucet in the house drips or hisses, the cause is usually pressure, not the fixtures, and a regulator or the water line may need checking. If the meter keeps turning with every fixture off, the leak is elsewhere. A new faucet will not fix either.

We also do not pretend a cartridge swap cures a corroded valve body. When the wall needs opening, we say so and quote it separately. Homes in Sunset and Clinton get the same straight answer.

Faucet and valve questions we hear

Do you handle faucet repair emergencies at night or on weekends in Clearfield?

Yes. The dispatch line runs 24/7 for burst stop valves, split supply lines, and faucets that will not shut off. We do not promise an arrival time, but we do tell you how to stop the water while you wait.

Is a dripping faucet really worth fixing?

Yes. EPA says one drip a second can waste more than 3,000 gallons a year. The repair is usually a cartridge or washer, so the part cost tends to be modest, and the leak can also stain a sink or erode a seat.

The valve under my sink will not turn. What should I do?

Do not force it. A stuck stop can snap its stem and start a leak. Close the main shutoff, then call a plumber to replace the stop and supply line at the same visit.

Should I replace the faucet or repair it?

Repair makes sense when the body and finish are good and the leak is at a cartridge or washer. Replace it when the body is pitted, the finish is peeling, or the repair cost nears a new faucet. The plumber will give you both options.

Does a faucet replacement need a permit in Clearfield?

Clearfield City says replacing plumbing fixtures does not need a permit, while new or replacement plumbing lines do. Permit rules vary, so ask the Clearfield City building department if your job moves or adds lines.

My outdoor spigot leaks inside the wall when I turn it on. Why?

A hose left attached over winter can freeze the line behind the spigot and split it. The split only leaks once the water is on. Close the interior shutoff and call us to replace it with a frost-proof bibb.

Can you fix a shower valve without opening the wall?

Often yes. Many cartridges come out from the front. If the valve body is corroded or the stem is seized, the wall must be opened, and the plumber explains that and quotes it before starting.
