How to Thaw a Frozen Pipe in Clearfield, UT

To thaw a frozen pipe, shut off the main water supply, open the faucet on the frozen line, and warm the pipe gradually, starting at the faucet and working back toward the ice. Use a hair dryer, heat lamp or warm towels. Never use an open flame.

If you see a crack, a wet wall or water where none should be, close the main and call a licensed plumber right away.

By the Clearfield Express Plumbing teamPublished October 15, 20266 min read

The thawing method, in order

Do these steps in this order. Skipping the first one is how a thawed pipe floods a house.

1Shut off the main

Find the main shutoff valve before you do anything else. If the pipe is already cracked, thawing will release water, and you want it stopped at the source. Keep a bucket and towels nearby.

2Open the faucet

Open the tap on the frozen line, hot and cold if it is a mixer. Melting ice needs somewhere to go, and an open tap relieves pressure as the pipe warms.

3Warm it from the tap backward

Start at the faucet end and move toward the frozen stretch. A hair dryer, heat lamp, warm towels, a space heater placed safely at a distance, or electric heat tape all work. Patience beats force here.

4Check for leaks, then restore water

When water runs freely, look along the pipe for cracks, wet drywall or drips. Turn the main back on slowly and watch. If anything leaks, close the main again and call.

Do not use a propane torch, a kerosene heater, boiling water or any open flame. Fast heat expands the ice and the metal at different rates, and open flame near a wall can start a fire inside it.

How often Clearfield pipes face a freeze

Ogden-area NOAA climate normals, from a station about 12 miles north of Clearfield, show 121 days a year with a low at or below 32 F. About 38 of those days reach 20 F or colder, and roughly 8 reach 10 F or colder. The median first freeze falls around October 22 and the median last freeze around April 22. That is a long season for any pipe in an unheated space.

Clearfield's own design criteria use a 30 inch frost line and a 10 F winter design temperature. Ground frost reaches that depth, and a pipe in an attic, garage wall, exterior wall or open crawlspace does not. These are the places to check first when a faucet goes quiet on a cold morning.

Clearfield City's water page also warns that freezing pipes can reduce pressure, so a weak flow in winter is a clue.

Source: FEDERAL, NOAA 1991 to 2020 climate normals for Ogden Hinckley Airport, and CITY, Clearfield City design criteria and water department pages.

First, work out which pipe is frozen

A trickle that stops, a faucet that coughs, or no water at one fixture while the rest work points to a localized freeze. If the whole house is dry, suspect the main or the meter area instead.

  • Check the coldest places first: exterior walls, the garage, an unheated crawlspace, under a kitchen sink on an outside wall.
  • Look for frost on a pipe or a bulge in it.
  • Feel pipes with the back of your hand. The frozen section is colder than the rest.
  • Try the hot and cold taps separately. If only cold works, the water heater side is fine.

A frozen hose bib or its supply line inside the wall is the most common culprit. Disconnect hoses in the fall so the bib can drain itself.

When thawing is the wrong call

Do not keep heating a pipe that has already split. If water sprays or pools when you open the main, close it and call. A thawed crack is a leak waiting to start, and a licensed plumber handles that on a burst pipe repair call.

Pipes inside a finished wall or ceiling are also a problem. You cannot safely heat them without opening drywall, and a heat source against a wall can ignite insulation or framing. A plumber can find where the pipe is frozen and where it has failed before you cut anything. Our leak detection work uses moisture meters and acoustic tools so the hole in the wall is the right size.

Signs the freeze has already done damage

Water stains on a ceiling below a bathroom, a pinging sound in the wall, a water meter that keeps moving when every tap is off, or a sudden drop in pressure after the thaw. Any of these means the pipe is leaking, so close the main.

How to stop it happening again

Insulate exposed lines with foam sleeves and seal gaps around pipes where they pass through exterior walls. Utah State University Extension notes that insulation alone will not stop a prolonged freeze without heat or some flow, so open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during a hard freeze. A slowly dripping faucet is a last resort.

Keep the heat on in unoccupied rooms, and keep your gas meter and exhaust vents clear of snow and ice, as the gas utility advises. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility from outside.

Frozen pipe questions from Clearfield homes

How do I thaw a frozen pipe without breaking it?

Shut off the main, open the faucet, and warm the pipe slowly from the tap back toward the ice. Use a hair dryer, warm towels or a heat lamp at a safe distance. Fast heat stresses the pipe, so avoid boiling water and flames.

How many nights a year does Clearfield freeze?

Ogden-area NOAA normals show about 121 days a year with a low at or below 32 F, and about 38 days at or below 20 F. The median first freeze is around October 22. The station is about 12 miles north, so your own street may run a little different.

How long does a frozen pipe take to thaw?

It depends on the length of ice and the heat you can apply safely. Expect a gradual job, not a few minutes. If you cannot find the frozen section or the pipe is behind a wall, stop and call.

Will a frozen pipe always burst?

No. Many freeze without splitting, but ice expands and can crack a pipe, often at a fitting. The split may only show after thawing, when water flows. That is why you thaw with the main closed and check for leaks.

Should I leave a faucet dripping overnight?

It can help on a very cold night. Flow keeps water moving, which resists freezing. It is a last resort and wastes water. Insulation and a warm space around the pipe do more.

Can I thaw a pipe inside the wall myself?

Usually not. You cannot see the pipe, and a heat source against a wall is a fire risk. A plumber can locate the frozen or cracked spot first and open only what is needed.
