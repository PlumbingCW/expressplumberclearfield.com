Sump Pump Installation Cost in Clearfield, UT

Sump pump installation cost in Clearfield, UT depends mostly on whether a pit already exists, how the water will leave the house, and what the pump has to handle. A swap into an existing pit is a small job. A new pit cut through a finished slab with a new discharge line is not.

We do not print a single number here because a number without your basement attached would mislead you. What follows is how the price is built, where it splits into cheap and expensive paths, and what to ask on a quote.

By the Clearfield Express Plumbing teamPublished October 15, 20268 min read

The price is set by four questions

Start with the pit. If a basin is already in the floor and the lid and check valve are sound, the job is mostly a pump swap. If there is no pit, someone has to break out concrete, dig, set a basin, and patch the slab.

Next comes the discharge. A short run to a daylight outlet on a lot that slopes away is cheap. A long run, a buried line, or a spot where the water cannot go to the sewer adds trenching and fittings.

Then ask what the pump has to do. A modest pump for an occasional damp spot is a different purchase from a primary pump with a battery backup in a house that takes water every spring. Last, ask who does the work and whether the job needs a permit.

Clearfield rules that shape where the water can go

The North Davis Sewer District, which serves Clearfield, bars anything that carries storm water, surface drainage, groundwater or roof runoff into the sanitary sewer. Its 2025 ordinance specifically lists groundwater sump pump discharges. So the pump cannot be plumbed into a basement drain or a sewer cleanout, however convenient that looks, and a quote that assumes it should be questioned.

Clearfield's storm water program says only storm water should go down the storm drain, and treats anything else as an illicit discharge. The city page does not give sump pump instructions. Before anything is trenched toward the street, confirm the discharge point with Clearfield Public Works.

Clearfield's building code on subsurface water also matters for the pit. Footings must sit at least two feet above the highest water table, or the home needs a foundation drain that runs to a positive drainage system. If your house relies on a drain tile, that system is what feeds the pit, so the pump has to be sized to it.

Source: North Davis Sewer District 2025 ordinances (special district), Clearfield City storm water department page, and Clearfield City Code 10-1-4 (city).

What drives the cost of a sump pump install

Ask each contractor to name these factors in the quote instead of folding them into one lump sum.

Pit and basin work
A new pit means breaking out and patching concrete. An existing basin only needs cleaning and a new check valve.
Pump type and horsepower
Submersible pumps sit in the basin, and pedestal pumps sit above it. Higher flow ratings and stronger switches cost more up front but handle spring loads.
Discharge piping
Length, slope, fittings, trenching and a frost-safe outlet all add labor and parts.
Backup power and alarms
A battery backup or a water-powered unit and a high-water alarm protect the basement when the grid goes out during a storm.
Permits and access
Permit rules vary, so ask the Clearfield City building department. Tight crawl access or a finished room slows the work.

Homeowner policies often treat a sudden pump failure that floods a basement differently from slow seepage or wear. Many exclude groundwater unless you add a rider, so check your policy before you rely on it.

Why spring matters to Clearfield basements

Ogden-area NOAA normals give about 16.4 inches of precipitation a year. April brings roughly 1.97 inches and May about 2.17, and those months overlap with snowmelt. A pump that sat dry all winter is asked to run hard just as the ground thaws.

The Utah Geological Survey notes that shallow groundwater, generally within 10 feet of the surface, can occur anywhere in the state and can seep into basements. It recommends inspecting sump pump systems every year before the wet season. Book that check in March and you will not be buying a pump in a flood.

How to compare two quotes fairly

Make both contractors price the same scope. If one quote leaves out the check valve and the other includes it, the cheaper one is not cheaper. Ask for the pump model, the basin size, the discharge route, and the finish repair in writing.

Ask where the water will go and how that complies with the North Davis Sewer District rule. A confident answer names an outdoor outlet, not a floor drain. Ask also what happens when the pump fails, since a basement with one pump and no alarm has a single point of failure.

  • Is the pit new or reused, and what happens to the slab?
  • Which pump, how many gallons per minute at your discharge height?
  • Is there a check valve, and is it accessible?
  • Where is the outlet, and who confirms it is allowed?
  • Is a backup pump or alarm included or optional?
  • What is excluded, and what is the clear price before work starts?

When a sump pump is not the real fix

A pump moves water that has already collected. It does not stop wall seepage, a cracked foundation or a gutter that dumps onto the footing. If the basement stays damp with a working pump, the answer may be drainage, grading or interior sealing. Our basement waterproofing page explains how those fixes work together.

We also cannot promise a dry basement. A plumber can size the pump, set the pit and route the water, but a high water table in a wet year can still outrun a modest system.

Sump pump install questions

Can I drain my sump pump into the sewer in Clearfield, Utah?

No. The North Davis Sewer District ordinance prohibits connecting groundwater sump pump discharges to the sanitary sewer, and it also bars storm water and roof runoff.

The discharge has to go outside to an approved outlet. Confirm the exact outlet with Clearfield Public Works before trenching.

Does a sump pump installation need a permit in Clearfield?

The pages we reviewed do not state a permit rule for sump pumps. Clearfield lists new plumbing installations and replacement lines as permit work, but a swap into an existing pit may be treated differently.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Clearfield City building department before work starts.

Why does a new pit cost more than a replacement pump?

A new pit requires breaking the slab, digging, setting a basin, running pipe and patching concrete. A replacement only swaps the pump and valve.

The extra labor and the slab repair are why the two jobs sit on different branches of the quote.

Should I add a battery backup pump?

Storms that load the pit often knock out power. A battery or water-powered backup keeps the basement protected when the main pump has no electricity.

It is optional and it adds cost. The more finished space you have below grade, the stronger the case.

How often should a sump pump be checked?

Once a year before the wet season. Pour water into the pit, confirm the float rises, the pump starts, and water leaves through the outlet.

The Utah Geological Survey recommends annual inspection for exactly this reason. Spring is the stress test.

Can a plumber tell me the cost without seeing the basement?

Only a range of branches, not a price. Photos of the pit, the slab, the discharge and the electrical outlet help.

A visit lets the plumber measure the run and check for drain tile, then give a clear price before work starts.
