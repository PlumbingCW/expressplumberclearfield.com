Copper Pipe Leak Repair in Clearfield, UT

Copper pipe leak repair in Clearfield, UT starts with the water, not the pipe. Close the main shutoff, open a low faucet to drain the line, and dry the spot so you can see where the water actually comes from.

A pinhole, a split, and a failed joint each need a different fix. Here is how to tell them apart and when to call a plumber.

By the Clearfield Express Plumbing teamPublished October 15, 20267 min read

Do these three things in the first ten minutes

Find the main shutoff and turn it clockwise until it stops. Then open the lowest faucet in the house, which lets the pressure drain out of the line instead of out of the leak. If water is near an outlet or the panel, keep people away.

Next, wipe the pipe dry with a towel and watch it for a minute. A bead that forms in the same spot points to a pinhole. Water that runs along the pipe from a fitting points to a joint, so the real source may sit higher than the wet spot.

Three kinds of copper leak, three different fixes

Reading the damage tells you whether a small repair will last.

Pinhole leaks

A pinhole is a tiny pit that wears through the tube wall. You may see green crust, a damp patch on drywall, or a fine spray under pressure. One pinhole can be cut out and replaced. Several in one run usually mean the pipe is thinning everywhere.

Split or burst tubing

A lengthwise split usually comes from water that froze inside the pipe and expanded. The leak often shows up after the thaw, when ice that was plugging the crack melts. The fix is to cut out the damaged section and sweat in a new length, since a clamp over a long split rarely holds.

Failed joints and fittings

Soldered joints can weep when the solder was starved or the pipe has moved over the years. Compression fittings loosen from vibration. A joint that drips only when the water heater cycles may be reacting to thermal expansion.

Patch or replace the section?

One leak in a clean, firm pipe can take a small repair. Green crust along the tube, flaking, or a second leak within a few feet says the section is finished. A patch on worn copper tends to fail a foot downstream.

What a homeowner can do, and where the law draws the line

Utah licensing law exempts minor incidental repair of a fixture in a residential building. That exemption does not cover work that modifies culinary water piping, which is exactly what cutting into a leaking copper supply line does. For a split or pinhole in the pipe itself, the safe call is a licensed plumber.

You can still help. Shut the water off, protect belongings, and photograph the damage for your insurer. A push-fit coupling can stop a drip in an emergency. Treat it as a bridge until a plumber replaces the section.

How a plumber finds the real source

Water travels along copper, studs, and joists before it drips, so the wet spot on a ceiling can sit several feet from the leak. The plumber starts with a pressure check on the isolated line, then listens along the pipe and uses a moisture meter on the surface.

Acoustic and thermal tools narrow a hidden leak before anyone cuts drywall. Our leak detection page covers how that works. For a pipe that has already split, burst pipe repair is the matching service.

What drives the cost of a copper leak repair

Two copper leaks that look alike can cost very different amounts. These are the factors that move the quote.

Access
A leak in an open basement ceiling is quick. A leak behind tile or inside a finished wall adds opening, patching, and repainting.
Length of pipe affected
One short section is a small job. A whole run with pinholes means more copper, more fittings, and more time.
Pipe size and location
Larger supply lines and joints near the water heater take more work to cut, dry, and sweat.
Water damage
Wet drywall, insulation, and flooring have to be dried or removed, which is often more than the pipe repair.
Timing
Same-day and after-hours calls are scheduled differently, so ask when you call.

Homeowners policies usually cover sudden and accidental water damage and often exclude slow leaks and wear. A pinhole that has dripped for months may fall on the wear side, so read your policy and ask your insurer before the repair.

When this is not a copper repair

If the meter keeps spinning with every fixture off after you fix a visible leak, water is escaping somewhere else, perhaps underground or in a slab. We reach homes in Roy and Sunset as well as Clearfield, and the first step is always to isolate and test the line.

Copper leak questions Clearfield homeowners ask

Why does my copper pipe have a pinhole leak and who do I call?

Causes vary. No pinhole-cause data has been published for Clearfield water, so any single explanation would be a guess. A plumber examines the pipe wall, the fittings, and how many leaks are showing before naming a likely cause.

Call a licensed plumber, since cutting a supply line modifies culinary water piping, which sits outside the minor repair exemption.

Can I fix a copper pipe leak myself?

You can stop the water. The permanent repair means cutting the line and making sound joints, which Utah licensing law does not treat as minor fixture repair. A push-fit coupling can bridge an emergency.

Replace it with a proper repair soon.

How do I know if the leak is inside the wall?

Look for a stained ceiling, a warm floor spot, or bubbling paint. A meter that moves with every fixture off is another sign. A plumber confirms with a pressure test and listening gear.

Do not cut drywall at the stain, because the source is often higher or off to the side.

Will one pinhole mean more are coming?

Not always. One pinhole in a clean, firm pipe may be an isolated fault. Several in the same run, or green crust along the tube, mean the wall is thinning.

Replacing the section then beats repeated callouts.

What should I do while waiting for the plumber?

Keep the main closed. Drain the line from a low faucet and move belongings out of the wet area. Photograph the damage for your insurer.
