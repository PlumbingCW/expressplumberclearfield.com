How Long Does It Take to Install a Toilet?

A like-for-like toilet swap on a sound floor is one of the shorter plumbing jobs, and a plumber usually finishes it in a single visit. What stretches it is what sits under the old toilet: a cracked flange, a soft subfloor, or a drain that is not where the new bowl expects it.

This guide walks through each stage of a toilet install in a Clearfield, UT home, shows where time disappears, and says when the job is more than a swap.

By the Clearfield Express Plumbing teamPublished October 15, 20267 min read

The stages of a normal swap

The work follows the same order every time. The plumber shuts the supply valve, flushes the tank empty and sponges out what is left, then disconnects the supply line and unbolts the old bowl from the floor flange.

Next comes the part that decides the rest of the visit. With the old toilet lifted off, the plumber scrapes off the old wax ring, inspects the flange and checks that the floor around it is firm.

If everything is sound, a new wax ring or a gasket goes on, the bowl is set and bolted down, and the tank is mounted. Last come the supply line, a flush test and a check for weeping at the base and the valve.

  • Shut off water, empty the tank and bowl
  • Disconnect the supply line and lift the old toilet
  • Clean the flange and inspect the floor
  • Set the new bowl on a fresh seal and bolt it down
  • Connect the supply, flush several times and check for leaks

Where the extra time goes

A rusted or broken closet flange is the most common surprise. A flange is the ring that anchors the toilet to the drain, and when its slots are snapped, the bolts have nothing to bite and the bowl rocks.

Soft flooring is the second. A toilet that has leaked slowly for years leaves the subfloor around the base dark and spongy, and screws in rotten plywood do not hold. Cutting out and patching that wood takes more time than the toilet itself.

The third is a fitting mismatch. A new bowl with a different rough-in distance from the wall, or a shutoff valve that will not close, can turn a swap into a plumbing alteration.

Rebates and permits for Clearfield toilets

Many Clearfield homes are old enough to qualify for a rebate. About 39 percent of the city's housing was built before 1980, according to Census survey data, and the Utah Water Savers program pays up to $150 when a WaterSense toilet replaces one made before 1994. The limit is two toilets per home.

The details matter. You apply within 30 days of buying, send photos and proof of purchase, and the old toilet has to go to solid waste or recycling. Take the photos before the plumber hauls it off, and ask for the box label to be left on the new bowl so the model is readable.

Clearfield City's permit guidance says replacing a plumbing fixture does not need a permit, while new or replacement plumbing lines do. That is why a plain swap needs no paperwork and a relocation does. Rules change, so call the Clearfield City building department if your job moves pipes.

Source: Utah Water Savers rebate program (state, Division of Water Resources) and Clearfield City permit guidance (city). Housing age from the U.S. Census Bureau American Community Survey.

Why a newer bowl is worth the trip

WaterSense toilets use 1.28 gallons per flush or less, about 20 percent below the 1.6 gallon federal standard. Toilets from earlier decades can use up to 6 gallons, and EPA says replacing an old toilet saves nearly 13,000 gallons a year in an average home.

For a Clearfield household with a 1970s bathroom, that is a big difference on the water bill. It is also a reason to use the visit to fix a running tank or a wobble you have lived with for years. If the old bowl is cracked or weeping, a plumber doing toilet repair can tell you whether a repair or a new bowl makes more sense.

Bowl shape matters on the install side too. A one-piece toilet is heavy and needs two people. A two-piece model is easier to carry upstairs but has a tank gasket and bolts that must be seated evenly.

Doing it yourself and where to stop

Many homeowners can swap a toilet over a weekend, and Utah law allows minor repair of a fixture in a home without a licence. The state's exemption does not cover work that changes waste or vent piping, and it excludes first-time fixture installations.

So set a clear stopping point. If the flange is broken, the floor is soft, or the shutoff valve will not close, stop and call a plumber for plumbing installation instead of forcing the bolts.

Homes in Roy and Layton share one common trap, a bathroom where new tile left the old flange sitting low. A flange that is below the finished floor needs a spacer, and a wax ring alone will not seal it.

Toilet install questions

How long does it take to install a toilet?

A like-for-like swap is a short visit, usually done in a single trip. Rot, a bad flange or moving the toilet adds time. A plumber who checks the flange first can tell you early whether it will stay simple.

How do I get the $150 toilet rebate in Clearfield, Utah?

Utah Water Savers pays up to $150 for a WaterSense toilet that replaces one made before 1994. Apply within 30 days of purchase with photos and proof of purchase, and finish within 90 days. The old toilet must be disposed of through solid waste or recycling. Check the program page for current rules.

Do I need a permit to replace a toilet in Clearfield?

Clearfield's guidance says replacing a plumbing fixture needs no permit. New or replacement plumbing lines do. Moving a toilet to a new spot falls on the line side, so ask the Clearfield City building department before you start.

Should I replace the wax ring when I reset a toilet?

Yes. A used ring will not reseal reliably. A new seal is cheap compared with the floor damage a slow leak causes, so replace it every time the bowl comes off.

Why does my new toilet rock?

Usually the flange sits too low or the floor is uneven. Shims and a flange spacer can fix a small gap. A cracked flange or soft floor needs repair. Do not just crank the bolts.

When should I call a plumber instead of doing it myself?

Call if the flange is broken, the floor feels soft or the shutoff valve will not close. Call too if the toilet is moving to a new spot. Those jobs touch waste piping, which Utah law does not treat as minor repair. Dial (801) 207-7520.
