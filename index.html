How a call goes

How an emergency call goes from first ring to finished repair

Four things happen, in order.

01

You describe the problem

Say what you see and hear, such as where the water is and whether a drain is backing up. The dispatcher also asks for your cross street.

02

A licensed plumber is assigned

The dispatch line sends a licensed plumber and tells you what to do while you wait. For a leak, that means closing the shutoff valve. For a sewer backup, it means stopping all water use.

03

The plumber finds the cause first

Before any pipe is cut, the plumber checks the cause with a moisture check, a pressure test or a sewer camera inspection. Then you get the plan and the price.

04

Work starts after you agree

Repairs begin once you approve the price. The plumber tests the fix and explains what to watch next. If a permit is needed, you hear so up front.

Clearfield landmarks and what they mean for your call

Hill Air Force Base borders Clearfield on the east, and some households here live on military timelines. A failing water heater then carries a deadline, such as a lease inspection or a closing date. Tell the dispatcher the date.

Falcon Hill sits on the base along Interstate 15, yet its mailing address reads Clearfield 84015. The Freeport Center, the old Navy depot, is now a warehouse hub inside the city. A Clearfield address can be a business, so say so, and the plumber arrives ready for commercial plumbing.

Our dispatch address is on Legend Hills Drive, on the southeast edge of Clearfield near the Layton line. State Street is the main street, home to City Hall. Give the nearest State Street cross street so the dispatcher can place you.

A booster pump serves the Freeport Center and the area south of Antelope Drive, so water pressure can differ across that road. If pressure seems oddly high or low, tell the plumber which side you are on.

The Davis and Weber Counties Canal still carries Weber River water, and the Utah Geological Survey says shallow groundwater is likelier near waterways and shifts with irrigation. A damp basement wall in July may be groundwater, not a pipe. Ask for leak detection before spending on anything else.

Who owns which pipe in Clearfield

Clearfield City runs its own water system and buys most of its supply from Weber Basin Water Conservancy District. Sewer service comes from North Davis Sewer District, not the city. A water problem and a sewer problem can involve two different offices.

Where the district line ends and your private line begins is not spelled out on the pages we reviewed, so ask the district. The city says most repairs or alterations need a permit, so ask the Clearfield City building department.

Source: Clearfield City Public Works and Community Development (clearfield.city), and North Davis Sewer District (northdavissewer.gov).

Emergency plumber questions, answered plainly

Do you really take calls at night and on weekends?

Yes. The dispatch line runs 24/7, including holidays, and every call goes to a licensed plumber. We do not promise an arrival time, because traffic and weather change it.

What should I do while I wait for an emergency plumber?

Close the main shutoff valve if water is flowing, and stop using drains if sewage is rising. Keep people away from wet electrical outlets. Lift valuables off the floor.

Will I know the price before the work starts?

Yes. The plumber finds the cause first, then explains the repair and the price before anything is cut. Pipe depth, access and parts all affect cost, which is why a phone figure would be a guess.

Are your plumbers licensed, and how can I check?

Every dispatched plumber is licensed and insured. You can verify any Utah plumber with the Verify a License search from the Division of Occupational and Professional Licensing. Ask for a name and license class, then look it up.

Which towns do you serve besides Clearfield?

We dispatch to Layton, Syracuse, Clinton, Sunset and Roy. Clinton and Sunset share ZIP 84015 with Clearfield, so the dispatcher confirms your town.

What counts as a plumbing emergency?

Flooding, sewage rising in a drain, a split pipe, a leaking tank and no water at all are emergencies. A slow drain or dripping faucet can usually wait for a same-day appointment when available. If unsure, call and describe it.

Can you come the same day for something less urgent?

Often. Same-day appointments are offered when available, and a faucet repair or toilet repair is a typical fit. If today is full, you get the next opening.

Do you handle businesses, or only homes?

Both. Warehouses, shops and offices call about grease-clogged lines, failed restroom fixtures and backflow testing, covered under commercial plumbing.
