When a failed tank floods the floor

A tank that has rusted through can release dozens of gallons in the time it takes to find the shutoff. Close the cold inlet valve first. That one valve stops the refill, so the leak shrinks to whatever is already in the tank.

Next, kill the heat source. Flip the dedicated breaker on an electric unit or turn the gas control to off on a gas unit. Do not stand in water near the panel, and do not try to drain the tank until the power or gas is off.

Gas smell near the heater is a separate emergency: leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility from outside. For everything else, call (801) 207-7520. After hours we dispatch the same way we do at noon, and a plumber can plan the swap for the next available same-day slot.

The ten to fifteen year rule, and why it matters here

The Department of Energy puts storage tank life at about 10 to 15 years and tankless units at around 20. Clearfield's median house was built in 1989, which means many heaters in town are already on their second or third tank. The age stamped in the serial number is your best single piece of evidence.

A tank past 12 years that has started leaking, rumbling or turning water rusty is almost always a replace decision. A tank under 8 with a bad element or valve is almost always a repair.

A tank that leaks only at a fitting or valve is a repair even when the heater is old.

No hot water and no puddle: do not assume the tank is dead

Total loss of hot water with a dry floor is rarely a reason to replace on its own. The plumber checks breaker, reset button, gas control and thermocouple first. Only when those pass and the tank fails a heat test does the conversation move to a new unit.

In a house with a cold tap problem at just one fixture, the heater may be fine. A failed mixing cartridge is cheaper than a tank, and a faucet repair call is the right one.

Choosing the replacement: gas, electric, condensing or tankless

Like-for-like is the simplest path, because the gas line, venting or circuit already exists. A gas tank swaps for a gas tank, an electric for an electric, and the plumber reconnects water lines with new flexible connectors.

Gas storage tank

Most Clearfield homes with a gas meter already have this. Pay attention to the venting type, because an older atmospheric flue and a modern power vent unit do not swap one for one.

Electric tank

Needs a correctly sized circuit and breaker. Switching from gas to electric means new wiring, and switching the other way means a gas line and a flue, so ask for both options priced.

Condensing or hybrid

These cost more to buy and use less fuel. Condensing gas units vent through plastic pipe and drain condensate, which changes what the installer needs to run.

Tankless

A bigger upfront spend and a different set of needs: larger gas line, dedicated venting and regular descaling. The tankless water heater page covers it in full.

Permit and licensing facts for a Clearfield swap

Clearfield City's building permit guidance lists water heater installations or replacements among the work that needs a building permit. Replacing a plumbing fixture does not, but new or replacement plumbing lines do. Fees and the inspection steps were not published where we looked, so ask the Clearfield City building department before work starts and ask whoever quotes the job who is applying for the permit.

Utah law also limits what an owner can do. The minor repair exemption does not cover work on a gas appliance or combustion system, so replacing a gas water heater belongs to a licensed plumber. The city's 2026 design criteria list the 2024 International Plumbing Code as the adopted edition from July 1, 2026.

Source: Clearfield City building permit guidance and 2026 design criteria, and Utah Code 58-55-305.

What a replacement quote should spell out

Two quotes for the same tank can differ by far more than the tank. Make each one list these items so you can compare like with like.

  • Unit fuel, size and recovery rate, and the manufacturer warranty on tank and parts
  • New flexible water connectors, a new relief valve and a discharge pipe routed to a safe spot
  • Whether an expansion tank is needed and included
  • Seismic strapping, since Clearfield sits in Seismic Design Category D or D1
  • Venting or electrical changes required by the new unit
  • Permit handling and who pays for it
  • Haul away of the old heater

Utah's earthquake program calls strapping a water heater one of the best low-cost steps a household can take, because a tipped heater can start fires, flood a house and fill it with carbon monoxide.

What moves the price of a new water heater

The unit is only part of the number. You get a clear price before work starts, and these are the factors behind it.

Type and capacity
A forty gallon electric and a high output condensing gas unit are different products with different labor.
Venting and fuel changes
Moving from atmospheric to power vent, or from gas to electric, adds pipe, wiring or both.
Where it sits
Attic, crawlspace and tight closet installs take longer and may need a drain pan or access changes.
Code upgrades
Strapping, expansion tank, relief valve discharge and a proper gas connector are often missing on older installs.
Timing
A planned swap is easier to schedule than a weekend emergency, though the line takes both.

Insurance generally pays for sudden and accidental damage, such as a tank that bursts and soaks the floors, and not for the heater itself, which wears out. Ask your carrier about the water damage and keep photos.

Rebates worth checking before you buy

The gas utility's ThermWise program lists appliance rebates for qualifying gas water heaters. The page shows a smaller rebate for an ENERGY STAR gas storage tank and larger ones for condensing storage units and gas hybrids. The request has to be postmarked within six months of installation.

Amounts and end dates can change, and the page covers Utah, Idaho and Wyoming, so confirm the current figure with ThermWise before you choose a model. Picking the unit and filing the rebate is your job, but the plumber can tell you which models qualify.

When a new heater will not solve the problem

A new tank will not fix poor house pressure, a failed pressure reducing valve or a gas line that was undersized to begin with. We would rather find that out at the quote stage. If the issue is a slow leak in a wall or slab, a heater swap does nothing, and leak detection is the right service.

We do not install commercial boilers or process heaters under this service. For residential tanks and tankless units, our dispatch also reaches nearby homes in Layton and Syracuse.

Replacing a water heater: common questions

Do you handle water heater replacement emergencies at night or on weekends in Clearfield?

Yes. The dispatch line runs 24/7 and a licensed plumber is sent to failed tanks and flooding. We book the first same-day or after-hours slot open and plan the swap from there. We do not state an arrival time, because it depends on where the plumber is when you call.

How do I know my water heater is too old to repair?

Age is the first clue. The Department of Energy puts storage tank life at about 10 to 15 years, so one beyond that is living on borrowed time. Rust at the base, water from the shell and repeated service calls all point the same way.

A plumber can read the date off the serial number in about a minute.

Can a leaking water heater be fixed instead of replaced?

It depends on where the water comes from. A drip from a valve or a connector is a repair. Water from the tank shell is not. The steel has corroded through, and no patch holds.

How long does a water heater replacement take?

A like-for-like swap on an accessible heater is usually a single-visit job. A fuel change, new venting or a tight attic can stretch it. The plumber will tell you the plan before starting. Cold showers during the work are normal, and hot water returns once the new tank has heated.

Does my water heater replacement need a permit in Clearfield?

Clearfield City's guidance lists water heater installations or replacements as work that needs a building permit. Fees and inspection details were not published on the page we reviewed, so ask the Clearfield City building department.

Ask whoever quotes the job to say who is pulling the permit before you agree.

Should I upgrade to tankless when my tank fails?

Tankless units last longer and use less energy, but cost more up front and need descaling. Clearfield buys most of its water from Weber Basin, which reports a district-wide hardness of 10 to 18 grains per gallon. That is an argument for planning a flush schedule, or a softener, if you go tankless.

What should I do with the old heater and the water on the floor?

Keep the power or gas off until the plumber arrives. Photograph the leak, the tank label and any damage for your insurance. Mop up with towels, and keep children and pets away from the standing water.
