Emergency or planned: how to decide

A job is an emergency when waiting makes damage worse or leaves your home unsafe or unusable. Water you cannot stop, sewage coming up through a floor drain, and a tank that has lost all its hot water on a cold night all qualify. Call (801) 207-7520 at any hour, because dispatch runs 24/7.

A job is planned when you can live with it for a few days. A dripping faucet, a toilet that runs, a water softener you want, or a repipe you have been meaning to price all fall here. You can schedule these for a normal day and compare what is involved.

Some jobs start as one and become the other. A slow drain is planned work on Monday and a backup on Friday if you ignore it. If you are unsure, shut off the water to the affected fixture, then call and ask.

  • Emergency: burst pipe, active leak you cannot isolate, sewage backup, no heat for water on a freezing night
  • Planned: dripping fixtures, slow but working drains, upgrades, inspections, installations
  • In between: a leaking heater that you can shut off at the valve, which is urgent but not frantic

First moves while you wait for the plumber

Know where your main water shutoff is before you need it. Turn it clockwise to stop water to the whole house, and stop using drains if sewage is involved. Keep people and pets away from wet areas near outlets and appliances.

If you smell gas, do not search for the cause. Leave the building and call 911 or your gas utility from outside.

How pricing works for plumbing work

We do not publish flat prices because two jobs with the same name can differ a lot. What we do promise is a clear price before work starts, so you can say yes or no with the number in front of you.

Access
A pipe in an open basement ceiling is quicker to reach than one behind tile or under a slab, and the labor reflects that.
Pipe material and age
Copper, PEX, galvanized steel and cast iron each need different fittings and techniques, and old pipe can break when touched.
Scope of the fix
Clearing a clog, replacing a part and replacing a whole line are three different jobs even when the symptom looks the same.
Parts and fixtures
A basic valve and a premium one are not the same cost, and a water heater's size and fuel type change the equipment bill.
Timing
After-hours calls and urgent work involve different scheduling than a planned visit booked ahead.

Home insurance often responds to sudden and accidental water damage and not to wear and tear, so read your policy before you assume either way. The repair of the worn part itself is commonly the homeowner's cost.

Where to start if you have only one question

Homeowners with old water heaters usually start at water heater replacement once the tank leaks, since a corroded shell cannot be patched. Buyers of older homes often begin with a sewer camera inspection so they know the condition of the line before closing.

If a drain keeps clogging after you clear it, hydro jetting scours the pipe wall instead of poking a hole through the blockage. For a remodel, plumbing installation covers new lines and fixtures, and the right order of work matters more than the order of purchases.

We do not treat septic systems or perform electrical or gas appliance work beyond the plumbing connection. If your problem sits outside our scope, the plumber will say so rather than guess.

Choosing a plumbing service: your questions

How do I know which plumbing service I need?

Start with what you see, hear or smell, then use the symptom table above. A slow drain points to drain cleaning, an unexplained water bill points to leak detection, and a cold shower points to the water heater.

If two services seem possible, call (801) 207-7520. The plumber will ask a few questions and confirm on site before quoting.

Are all of these services available around the clock?

The dispatch line is open 24/7 and urgent jobs such as bursts, backups and active leaks are treated as emergencies. Planned work like installations, repiping and water softeners is usually scheduled for regular hours.

Same-day appointments are offered when available. We do not promise a set arrival time.

Do you give a price before the work begins?

Yes. You get a clear price before work starts, based on access, materials, scope and timing. You can accept it or decline before any repair begins.

Does the plumber need a permit for my job?

Some work, such as water heater installation or a new line, may involve a permit. Permit rules vary, so ask the Clearfield City building department for what applies to your address.

The plumber can tell you what the job involves and help you plan around inspections where they apply.

Yes. Commercial plumbing covers restrooms, leak calls, backflow testing, grease traps and storm drains for shops, offices and warehouses. After-hours calls go through the same 24/7 line.
