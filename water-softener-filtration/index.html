Water Softener and Filtration in Clearfield, UT

Water softener installation in Clearfield, UT means plumbing a treatment tank into your main water line so hardness minerals are swapped out before they reach the heater and fixtures. Filtration is a separate job that targets taste, sediment or chlorine. Not every home needs either one. Start with a test, then call (801) 207-7520, and the 24/7 line will book a visit.

Test the tap before you buy a tank

Clearfield City's published water quality report lists no hardness figure, so nobody can honestly tell you a Clearfield number from a brochure. Weber Basin Water Conservancy District, which sells the city most of its water, reports a district-wide hardness range of 10 to 18 grains per gallon (171 to 308 parts per million). That is a supplier range, not a reading from your tap.

Get your own number with a lab test or a hardness test kit from your kitchen faucet, and ask Clearfield City for its annual water quality report. The result decides the size of the unit. A softener sized to a guess either runs out of capacity early or wastes salt.

What Clearfield City tells you about its water

The city's 2025 water quality report names its sources as two city wells, the Reservoir Well and the Hill AFB Well, plus Weber Basin water. Because the mix changes with demand, your tap can differ from a neighbor's. Total dissolved solids ran 324 to 400 parts per million. That counts all dissolved minerals, so it is not hardness, and you should not read it as a softener sizing number.

Sodium measured 17.7 to 39.8 parts per million, which matters because an ion exchange softener adds sodium as it removes calcium and magnesium. If someone in the house watches sodium, ask your doctor before you commit. The city also samples chlorine residual daily, so a carbon filter will help if chlorine taste is your complaint.

Source: Clearfield City 2025 Consumer Confidence Report and Clearfield City Public Works water department page.

What hard water does inside your plumbing

Scale builds where water is heated or evaporates. You see it as chalky rings on glass, crust on aerators and showerheads, and a dishwasher film that returns after cleaning. You hear it as popping or rumbling from a tank heater with sediment under the burner or element.

Tankless units are more sensitive. The federal Department of Energy says scale in the heat exchanger can restrict flow and shorten the unit's life. It also notes that some manufacturers require a softener when hardness runs above roughly 8 to 14 grains per gallon. Weber Basin's range sits above that threshold, so confirm your own number before you buy. See water heater repair for scale-related failures.

How a softener installation goes

The plumber picks a spot on the main line after the shutoff and before the water heater branch, usually in a basement, garage or utility room. The spot needs a nearby drain, a standard outlet and enough room to reach the brine tank.

Bypass and hookup

A bypass valve lets you isolate the unit for service without shutting off the house. Copper, PEX and older galvanized lines each take a different connection method. If the pipe near the main is badly corroded, repiping may come before any treatment.

Drain and discharge

Regeneration sends brine and rinse water to a drain through an air gap. North Davis Sewer District sets what may enter the sewer, and we could not confirm a published rule on softener discharge, so ask the district if your house has unusual drainage.

Setup and permit

The plumber sets hardness, capacity and regeneration, then checks for leaks under pressure. Clearfield City treats new plumbing lines as permit work and fixture swaps as exempt. Permit rules vary, so ask the Clearfield City building department.

When a softener stops softening

Water conditioner repair usually starts with a short list. The brine tank may be empty, or salt may have bridged into a crust over a pocket of water. A clogged injector, a stuck valve, a tired resin bed or a dead control board can also be the cause.

The first check is cheap. Look inside the tank, break any crust, and run a manual regeneration. If hardness comes back within a week, the plumber tests the outlet water and the valve. Some control boards need manufacturer parts, which can add time.

Filtration without the hype

Filters trap what their media can reach and nothing else. Sediment cartridges catch grit, carbon catches chlorine taste and some organics, and reverse osmosis cleans a single drinking tap. Cartridges expire, and an overdue one can foul water instead of cleaning it.

Look for third party certification to the standard for the specific contaminant you care about. If your concern is a health issue, send a sample to a certified lab first. The city's water quality line on the clearfield.city site handles questions about the municipal supply. The plumber on our plumbing installation visits can fit a filter at the sink or the main.

What decides the cost of a softener or filter job

No honest figure exists until your hardness and layout are known. These five factors move the total.

Unit capacity
Household size and measured hardness set the grain capacity, and bigger tanks cost more.
Existing pipe
Copper, PEX and corroded galvanized lines need different fittings and sometimes repair first.
Drain and power
A far drain, a missing outlet or no air gap adds plumbing and electrical work.
Filter add-ons
Carbon, sediment and reverse osmosis stages each add parts and replacement cartridges.
Permit and removal
A permit, if needed, and hauling an old unit add steps and time.

Homeowner insurance rarely pays for a water treatment upgrade, since it is an improvement, not damage. A sudden leak from a failed tank or valve may be covered depending on your policy's sudden and accidental wording, while wear and corrosion generally are not.

What a softener cannot fix

A softener does not remove bacteria, lead or other contaminants, and it does not cure low pressure or rusty water from a failing main. Those need a water test, a pressure check, or a look at the service line. See water line repair if pressure is the real complaint.

Softener and filter questions from local homeowners

How hard is the water in Clearfield?

The city's own report does not state hardness, so no Clearfield figure is published. Weber Basin, its main supplier, reports a district range of 10 to 18 grains per gallon. Test your tap with a lab kit or ask the city for its annual report before sizing any unit.

Do I need a softener if I have a tankless water heater?

Often, but test first. The Department of Energy says scale can restrict flow in tankless heat exchangers, and some makers require a softener above a hardness threshold. Check the manufacturer's rules for your model, then compare them with your own water test.

Will a softener make my water unsafe for someone limiting sodium?

An ion exchange softener adds sodium as it removes hardness, and Clearfield's city water already carries some. People on sodium-restricted diets should ask a doctor, and may prefer an unsoftened drinking tap with the rest of the house softened.

Does a salt-free conditioner work as well as a softener?

Not the same way. It changes how scale forms so it clings less, but it does not remove calcium or magnesium. If you want soft water for soap, skin and spotting, an ion exchange unit does that more fully.

Can you repair a softener you did not install?

Often yes, starting with the salt, injector and valve checks. Some control boards need manufacturer parts, which can add time. We will tell you plainly if a unit is past worth fixing.

Does a softener installation in Clearfield need a permit?

Clearfield City treats new plumbing lines as permit work and fixture swaps as exempt, and a softener adds fittings to the main. Permit rules vary, so ask the Clearfield City building department before work begins.
